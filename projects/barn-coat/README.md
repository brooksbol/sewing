# Barn coat

**Status (2026-11-04):** Pattern fitting and construction experiments; no lining yet.

## Design intent

A long, straight, practical but refined barn/work coat: Ralph Lauren Western/workwear character with Tractor Supply usefulness. Reference: a tan Ralph Lauren workwear coat with contrasting dark-brown corduroy collar, upper flap pockets, large lower pockets, traditional buttons, and a generous straight body. Fit over a sweater; err toward longer length. No yoke, waist darts/suppression, zipper, or tucked-in styling. Split center-back body panels are intended.

**Finished fabric:** military twill, described as nicer than the relatively heavy sale canvas from Hobby Lobby used for the first prototype. Target is a soft, durable medium-weight twill/canvas rather than stiff heavy duck. Fully lined eventually; flannel body and slick Bemberg/cupro sleeve lining have been considered but not finalized.

## Current pattern

**Winifred Aldrich — basic overgarment block**, drafted to the maker's measurements. This supersedes the experimental shirt-derived barn coat draft. Shoulder geometry and overall one-piece sleeve fit are dramatically improved.

**Current fitting observations over a sweater:**
- Still somewhat snug in sleeves, more noticeably snug at abdomen.
- Localized poof in sleeve cap **immediately behind shoulder seam at crown**, not down the back of upper arm.
- Sleeve otherwise works well; reluctance to change the cap curve.
- Forward sleeve rotation is a candidate, even if sleeve underarm seam no longer meets bodice side seam. Rotating sleeve affects seam alignment, but alignment is not a mandatory requirement.
- Also considered: slight correction of front bodice outer shoulder point / overall shoulder balance, based on previous shirtmaking experience. Not yet confirmed.

### Curve measurements supplied 2026-11-04

| Segment | Armscye | Sleeve cap | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Front | 34.5 cm | 37 cm | +2.5 cm |
| Back | 35.5 cm | 38 cm | +2.5 cm |
| **Total** | **70 cm** | **75 cm** | **+5 cm** |

These are *measure-along-the-curve* figures. **Whether measured on stitching lines or cutting edges has not been established**, so 5 cm should not yet be asserted to be verified *sewn-in cap ease*. Relative difference is ~7.1% of the 70 cm armscye reference. Equal front/back totals do not establish equal *local* ease distribution.

The maker reports sewing the one-piece sleeve's long seam with **2 cm seam allowance**, versus **1 cm at bodice side seam**, which helps insertion fit cleanly with little bunching and yields a crisp visible armscye/topstitch. This changes sleeve circumference and cap endpoints; it must not be assumed to remove exactly all 5 cm of measured difference. The apparent accuracy of the paper geometry and applicability of Aldrich's formulas at unusually large measurements remain open questions, not findings.

## Construction work in progress

- Prototype #1: relatively heavy Hobby Lobby canvas, useful for seams, bulk, machine behavior, and structural fitting; next fitting toile planned in light muslin for body and sleeves.
- Structural machine setup being tried: heavy thread, **2.8–3.0 mm stitch length**, medium-heavy presser-foot pressure.
- Shell seams presently neither flat-felled nor serged, because intended lining will conceal seam allowances. Lining attachment remains untested.
- New pocket experiments: **buttoned upper pockets** and **slide-in lower pockets**. Exact lower-pocket opening architecture not yet established.
- Front placket/facing and hem experiment: matching canvas sewn right sides together and turned inward, analogous to construction of shirt cuffs; intended to create a clean edge and lining attachment point. Corners, bulk management, and lining ease not yet settled.
- Decorative stitching tradition: symmetrical pairs 1/4 in to either side of open structural seam; folded-edge stitch close to fold plus second line 1/4 in away, using heavier contrasting top thread. See [construction notes](construction.md).

## Next fitting decisions

1. Resolve abdomen and modest sleeve snugness over sweater without undoing improved Aldrich shoulder/armscye geometry.
2. Diagnose rear-crown poof using reversible sleeve orientation and local ease distribution before changing sleeve cap curve.
3. Clarify whether curve measurements were taken on seam lines or cutting edges; mark shoulder/pitch/balance reference points.
4. Verify adjustments in heavier material before cutting the military twill.
5. Prototype pocket shape, turned facing/hem corners, and later lining assembly.
