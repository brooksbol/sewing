# Barn coat — construction and equipment

## Fabric and lining

- Final shell: military twill (preferred to first prototype's sale heavy canvas).
- Intended medium-weight, resilient workwear character; not rigid duck.
- Prototype #1: relatively heavy Hobby Lobby canvas for stitching, seam behavior, and fit.
- Future fitting muslin: light muslin body and sleeves.
- Coat to be fully lined, but **lining not yet constructed**.
- Body flannel vs Bemberg/cupro under discussion; smooth sleeve lining preferred for wearing over denim/flannel shirts.
- Shell and lining conceived as separately assembled layers joined at boundaries, not stitched together along every seam. Provide movement ease; avoid restrictive sleeve lining.

## Stitching grammar

**Established from successful denim shirt:**
- Structural seams: strong polyester; historically approximately 2.5 mm stitch length.
- Current heavy canvas experimentation: heavier thread at **2.8–3 mm**, medium-heavy presser-foot pressure.
- Decorative: heavier Gütermann Denim top thread (medium brown selected for coat), thin polyester bobbin, approximately **4 mm** stitch length.
- Symmetrical seam treatment: structural plain seam pressed open; decorative lines **1/4 in each side** of seam (1/2 in apart). Examples: split center back, longitudinal sleeves, side seams.
- Directional folded edges: first decorative stitch approximately **1 mm** from fold, second **1/4 in further**.
- Decorative topstitching is not a substitute for structural stitching.

**Current seam treatment:** no universal flat-felling or serging for enclosed shell seams; lining will hide allowances. Grade/trim bulk where needed, watch fraying through fitting and washing.

## Sleeve and movement

- One-piece Aldrich sleeve is current; earlier two-piece workwear sleeve is historical.
- Sleeve geometry generally successful, with rear crown poof and slight sleeve snugness outstanding.
- Original sleeve experiments considered 5 cm cuff turnback (marks 5 and 10 cm from raw edge), parallel-sided last 10–15 cm to prevent turnback bunching, and a securing stitch above cuff fold. These are **historical proposals** and have not been confirmed for the current Aldrich coat.

## Pockets and edges

- Experimenting with buttoned upper pockets and slide-in lower pockets; final pocket architecture and scale are open.
- Matching shell fabric sewn right-sides-together then turned inward to finish front placket/facing and lower hem, like a shirt cuff. Intended inner attachment edge for lining. Still experimental: investigate front/hem corner intersections and bulk, as well as lining slack.

## Singer Quantumlock 4 serger — historical accepted sample

- Singer Quantumlock 4; identification plate says **Model Series 14U**; precise subtype not confirmed.
- Width dial accessible beneath free-arm cover, avoiding knife screw adjustment.
- Width approximately **5 mm**; both looper tensions loosened roughly one dial step; needle tensions unchanged.
- Shorter of two trial stitch lengths produced preferred flat, well-covered edge sample on medium canvas twill.
- Baseline retained for other projects/material trials, **not** an instruction to serge this coat's lined shell seams.

## Open assembly details

Front facing/lining connection, collar geometry and attachment, lower hem/corners, pocket bags, sleeve lining/cuffs, hardware/button choice, and final assembly order remain undecided.
