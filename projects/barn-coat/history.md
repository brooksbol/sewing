# Barn coat — superseded experiments and retained lessons

**Historical only.** The following describes work before adoption of Winifred Aldrich's basic overgarment block. Do **not** use the shirt-derived draft as current pattern authority.

## Original adapted shirt block

The maker had successful custom shirt blocks, including a fitted shirt with a yoke, back pleat ease, and close armscyes. Initial barn coat attempt extended those patterns, eliminated the yoke, and tried larger cap/armhole/underarm dimensions. This introduced interacting fit problems:

- Front chest and lower back tight over sweater; lower back lost useful circumference when pleat intake was incorrectly removed from entire center back.
- Back neck too wide and positioned rearward; shoulders/upper back too tight.
- Lower armhole and bicep too tight.
- Sleeve cuff migrated up past wrist on arm elevation.
- Lowering the armscye alone was not a reliable solution; excessive armhole drop can worsen mobility.

The retained lesson is that a coat block has its own shoulder, neck, chest, armscye and sleeve geometry; simply scaling a fitted shirt draft is not equivalent.

## Experimental custom two-piece sleeve

A top/cap-side and pit/undersleeve were developed to independently manage upper-arm fullness, elbow-to-wrist taper, and workwear reach. A shallow curve replaced an overly deep lower division on the cap-side piece; the **outer cap curve was not flattened**. A trial had a deliberately generous ~16 in cuff circumference, not a final spec. The under piece could be narrowed below elbow, preserving a parallel last 10–15 cm to ease cuff turnback.

Test emphasis was dynamic: raise arms forward/side/overhead, cross arms, simulate steering, record cuff migration and body lift. The architecture did not automatically guarantee mobility. The entire experimental sleeve direction was superseded when the Aldrich block markedly improved fit.
