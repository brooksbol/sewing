# Barn coat fitting log

## 2026-11-04 — Aldrich basic overgarment, canvas prototype

**Source:** maker's statements in ongoing sewing discussion; not an independently measured pattern audit.

**Established observations**
- Abandoned DIY shirt-derived coat draft and started again from Aldrich's basic overgarment block.
- Shoulder geometry and sleeve fit improved drastically.
- Fit standard remains a sweater under the coat.
- Snugness remains: slightly in sleeves, more significantly at abdomen.
- A localized poof sits directly behind shoulder seam near sleeve-cap top; not a long fold down back arm.
- The one-piece sleeve works well overall; maker prefers not to alter cap geometry without evidence.
- Seam alignment sleeve-underarm to bodice-side is optional, so forward sleeve rotation can be tested.

**Measured curve lengths (measurement path not yet clarified):**
- Front armscye: 34.5 cm; back armscye: 35.5 cm.
- Front sleeve cap: 37 cm; back sleeve cap: 38 cm.
- Difference: 2.5 cm front + 2.5 cm back = 5 cm overall.

**Construction observation:** sewing the sleeve long seam at 2 cm allowance versus body side seam at 1 cm permits a notably clean cap insertion and crisp front armscye topstitch, with little visible bunching. Do not equate this with proof of exact cap ease removed.

**Competing explanations — not yet proven**
- Concentrated excess near rear crown.
- Sleeve pitch/orientation requiring modest forward rotation.
- Bodice shoulder balance: perhaps outer front shoulder point needs to drop slightly, as experienced in dress shirts.
- Paper drafting/measurement discrepancy.
- Aldrich fixed or proportional formulas being less accurate at this size.

**Provisional reversible test:** preserve cap shape. Check shoulder-seam position on body, mark seam lines and balance points, baste alternate sleeve rotation without requiring underarm seams to align, and separately test cap ease distribution. Compare hanging shape, reach, and cuff migration over sweater. Avoid changing multiple variables at once.

**Next toile:** light muslin for bodice/sleeves, with caution that it will not replicate heavy canvas/twill drape.

## Earlier — first coat experiments

A shirt-derived pattern initially had tight fronts, lower back, shoulders, lower armhole, and biceps over sweater; back neck wide/too far aft; sleeves rode up on arm elevation. A custom two-piece sleeve was explored for adjustability and mobility. That experiment was superseded by the Aldrich draft; see [history.md](history.md).
