# Brother CS-6000i

**Current status (direct owner update, 2026-11-04):** Owned and used **only for buttonholes**. The Juki DDL-8700 now handles approximately 99% of stitching. The former general-purpose/light-muslin role below is historical, not the current workflow.

## Discussed characteristics

- Computerized domestic machine; conventional drop feed, no integrated walking feed.
- Maximum straight-stitch length reported as **5 mm**; maximum stitch width **7 mm** (discussion figures, not reverified here).

## Historical workshop use

Practiced flat-felled seams, double-row stitching, laundered/pressed painter's canvas drop cloth, grommet-corner reinforcements, and multiple overlapping folds.

**Observed limitations:** repeated E6 errors at thick seam intersections, requiring reset; occasional handwheel assistance; inconsistent feeding and precision at bulky reinforced corners. The thread concluded it was not suitable for *repeated heavy multilayer canvas* work, although useful for muslin and lighter garments.

Do not generalize this outcome to all sewing machines or assume it reflects a particular fault.

## Current buttonhole role

The automatic buttonhole system has worked inconsistently on fine cotton broadcloth. The plastic foot was viewed as flimsy, but no cause has been established. See [buttonhole reference](../buttonholes.md) for sizes, tests, and contemplated improvements; no dedicated replacement machine is documented.

## Needle finding — 2026-11-04

**Direct maker observation:** **Microtex 70/10** is the only needle type/size tested so far that does **not** jam or bend during the Brother's buttonhole operation. This is the current proven needle choice for this machine's buttonholes; other needles the maker tried jammed or bent. Specific alternative needles and brands tested were not enumerated.

Single-layer heavy canvas buttonholes have already been tested. Double-canvas placket tests and alternate thread weights are planned, **not yet reported as successful**. Preserve Microtex 70/10 as the baseline test variable, rather than replacing it with a generic 80/12 recommendation.
