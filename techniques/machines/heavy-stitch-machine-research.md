# Heavy-woven straight-stitch machine research

**Status:** Historical comparison and shopping preferences as of supplied thread export (2026-11-04), **not purchase decisions or independently verified product specifications**.

## Intended capability

Heavy canvas/denim/duck, multiple bulky layers, precise long straight stitches, reliable feeding at reinforced corners, mechanical serviceability, controllable low-speed/high-torque operation, and portability between basement and temporary barn workshop. Prefer dedicated straight-stitch and integrated walking/compound feed. Original stitch-length aspiration **6–10 mm, preferably near 10 mm**.

Feed distinction: accessory walking foot on a drop-feed machine is not identical to an engineered walking-feed system. Compound/triple feed coordinates needle, feed dogs and walking foot. Consistent layer transport, rather than only penetration force, was the main concern.

### Juki DDL-8700 opportunity

Used complete $900 setup noted (head, table, servo, chair, accessories). Price appeared plausible, but drop feed, ~5 mm discussed stitch length, and permanent footprint detracted. **No purchase recorded.**

### Sailrite LS-1 preference

Portable, mechanically simple straight-stitch-only machine, narrower foot than LSZ-1 for corner visibility, and manufacturer support. WorkerB power input was discussed as about **375 W rated**, without measured peak current. Tradeoff: ~6 mm maximum stitch length falls below the desired 8–10 mm look. **Candidate only.**

## Evaluation tests before purchase

Heavy seam intersections, actual layered reinforced corners, flat-felled seams, feeding consistency, controllable single-stitch/pivot work, usable stitch length and straightness. No results from testing a candidate machine are recorded.
